# AI Strategy — Teensurance AI

## Strategic role of AI in the product

AI is the *explainer and orchestrator* of a deterministic, sourced system — not the source of truth. Teensurance's core rule is: **AI explains. Rules establish requirements.** The Journey Graph, Requirement Graph, and GUARD safety kernel are deterministic; the AI layer (T and the T-Crew) sits on top of them to interpret user intent, explain state, and route to the right specialist capability.

This keeps two categories of risk separate and independently manageable:

1. **Regulatory/factual risk** — handled by a sourced, versioned Requirement Graph, never by free-generation from the LLM.
2. **Behavioral/safety risk** — handled by GUARD, a deterministic policy layer, not by prompting the LLM to "be safe."

## Orchestration architecture

```
User → T → Intent/Context → GUARD → Specialist Agent
     → Domain Service / Tool → Deterministic Rules → Result
     → GUARD → T Explanation
```

- The LLM never has direct, unrestricted database access.
- Every proposed agent action is evaluated by GUARD before and after execution.
- Structured outputs are used for anything that drives UI or business logic.
- Agent assignment, tool/action calls, decisions, safety decisions, and evidence references are persisted for auditability.
- Hidden chain-of-thought is never persisted; only concise decision rationale/provenance is stored where the product needs it.

## The single-assistant model (T)

Users interact with one assistant, T, rather than selecting among specialist agents. T should feel supportive, concise, intelligent, age-appropriate, non-patronizing, and actionable. Internally, T routes to the T-Crew (VIBE, READY, ACE, MILES, CRUZE, GO, COVER — see `AGENTS.md`) based on journey state and intent. This keeps the product surface simple while the backing system is compositional and auditable.

## GUARD — the safety kernel

GUARD is the highest-priority piece of the AI architecture. It is explicitly **not** "an LLM being asked to behave" — it is a policy/decision layer that every proposed action must pass through, returning one of: `ALLOW`, `DEFER`, `REQUIRE_PARENT`, `REQUIRE_CONSENT`, `REQUIRE_VERIFICATION`, `REQUIRE_OFFICIAL_SOURCE`, `ESCALATE`, `DENY`.

GUARD evaluates: driving state, user age, role, jurisdiction, data sensitivity, consent, guardian relationship, agent permission, notification policy, evidence requirement, regulatory boundary, and safety classification — producing an auditable `SafetyDecision` record every time.

**Non-negotiable behaviors enforced by GUARD:**
- During an active drive, T conversations, achievements, rewards, insurance offers, educational interactions, unnecessary notifications, manual data entry, social features, marketing, and interactive coaching are all disabled or deferred.
- No agent, reward, notification, growth objective, or commercial objective may be structured to encourage behavior that increases driving risk.
- Agents cannot act outside their declared `allowed_actions` / `prohibited_actions`, and cannot access data outside their declared `data_permissions` / `minor_data_permissions`.

## Grounding and anti-fabrication strategy

- Regulatory/requirement content is served from the versioned Requirement Graph (`Jurisdiction → Requirement → RequirementVersion`, each with a source, effective dates, and verification status). If verified data isn't available for a jurisdiction, it is explicitly marked `UNVERIFIED / DEMO DATA` — never silently generated by the model.
- COVER (insurance-facing AI) never fabricates quotes, guarantees savings, claims underwriting eligibility, or binds coverage. It sticks to education, a household insurance snapshot, and a preparation checklist.
- Agents that need authoritative information outside their own confidence declare `REQUIRE_OFFICIAL_SOURCE` or escalate to a named human authority (parent, supervisor, licensed insurance professional, driving instructor, government authority, emergency services) rather than answering as if they were that authority.

## Data and privacy posture for AI

- Journey data (progress, milestones, evidence) is architecturally separate from insurance-rating/telematics data; the AI layer cannot silently move data between the two without explicit, purpose-specific consent.
- Data minimization is a default: driving-hour tracking does not assume permanent GPS route history is required.
- Turning 18 is modeled as a lifecycle/permissions event that can change what the AI is allowed to access or share, not an edge case handled ad hoc.

## Observability for the AI layer

Key events instrumented: `journey_created`, `next_step_viewed`, `milestone_started`, `milestone_completed`, `drive_started`, `drive_ended`, `drive_submitted`, `drive_verified`, `drive_corrected`, `requirement_viewed`, `parent_action_completed`, `cover_started`, `agent_assigned`, `agent_handoff`, `safety_deferred`, `safety_denied`, `exception_created`. Unnecessary sensitive telemetry is not collected.

## What AI strategy explicitly avoids

- Optimizing any AI-driven surface for miles driven, drive count, or streaks.
- Using AI to manufacture urgency, competition, or engagement loops during or around driving.
- Letting AI-generated content stand in for a sourced regulatory or insurance fact.
- Exposing the agent/orchestration architecture, safety policy design, or Journey/Requirement Graph structure on any public-facing surface.

## Scout learning guidance

Scout recommends source-linked RoadReady concepts and explains validated answers.
Its contract is in `lib/roadready/agent.ts`. GUARD checks authenticated scope, consent,
driving state and permitted actions before the service executes; the result receives
an auditable decision. Scout cannot access the database directly or create regulatory,
insurance, eligibility or driver-ranking judgments. Recommendations sit below the
existing journey requirement displayed first in RoadReady. Minimal educational events
remain in household-scoped local storage; no telemetry is sent to an external vendor.
